## 1. A Month Later

Four weeks had passed since Kura and his friends stopped Sammy’s crew from pushing younger Handlers around. The district had not become peaceful exactly—District 4 never stayed quiet for long—but the Trouble Crew had stopped making trouble. Between customer jobs and the neighborhood practice sessions, Kura had barely found an evening to himself. The practice log beside his tools held losses as well as wins, and the latest repair bill had made him more careful about how long he kept Zerdata in the ring.

Kura had seen them around. Once, he found Sammy, Spy, and Slow in a little plaza, crouched beside three kids and a stubborn practice bot with one wheel jammed sideways. Sammy was holding the chassis steady while Spy gave directions no one had asked for. Slow sat cross-legged in the dust, patiently untangling a cable.

“You’re supposed to turn the wheel the other way,” Spy announced. “I know this model. I once repaired one blindfolded.”

“You never repaired one blindfolded,” Sammy said.

“I could have.”

The little bot gave a mournful squeak. Kura leaned over, spotted a bent axle pin, and showed the child how to straighten it. Sammy watched, pretending not to listen. When the bot rolled straight again, the kids cheered. Spy took a dramatic bow as though he had done the work himself.

It was not a grand change. It was a start.

Slow’s visits to the Ato Family Workshop became another part of the new routine. He would show up with Honey-Beam’s arm panel loose or a joint making a grinding sound, carrying the heavy AniBot as carefully as if it were made of glass. Kura fixed what he could and taught Slow how to spot the early signs of wear.

“I can pay you next time,” Slow said on his third visit.

“You said that last time,” Kura replied, tightening a shoulder bolt.

“Yeah. I’m still planning to.”

“Then bring the parts in before they break.”

Slow gave his sleepy, grateful smile. “That sounds easier.”

He lingered after the repair, watching Kura work and asking small questions about tools and wiring. He never quite asked if he could stay. Kura never told him to leave.

The shop had changed, too. The win over Sammy’s crew had brought curious customers, and word of Kura’s careful repairs had traveled farther than he expected. People began bringing in worn-out loaders, delivery bots, and old household units that other shops had refused.

One evening, Raya laid the day’s accounts beside the register and counted the receipts twice.

“Bills are covered,” she said, sounding as if she still didn’t quite trust the numbers. “And there’s something left after them.”

Kura looked up from a half-open sensor housing. “Enough to order the parts you wanted?”

“Enough to order the parts we need,” she corrected, smiling. “We’ll see about the rest.”

The extra money was not much, but it meant the lights could stay on without Raya calculating every hour of power. It meant Kura could think about something besides the next repair.

That was when Luk showed up at the shop with a travel brochure folded into his jacket pocket.

“You need a break,” he said, dropping the brochure on the counter.

“I’m standing right here,” Kura said. “You can just tell me.”

“District 9. Highlands. My parents have been talking about this little inn up there for years. Hot springs, mountain trails, good food. We leave Friday.”

“We?”

“Me and you.”

Kura glanced toward the back, where Raya was sorting invoices. “The shop—”

“Can survive two days without you,” Raya called from the storeroom. “Go. You’ve earned a little time away.”

“But the new parts—”

“Will still be there when you get back.”

Isabell had just come in to collect a repaired practice drone. She stopped at the counter, eyes bright. “You’re going where?”

Luk turned. “District 9. Just me and Kura.”

“That sounds wonderful! I’ll come too!”

“Wait. Who invited you?”

“I just did.” She looked at Kura. “If that’s all right?”

“Sure,” Kura said. “It’ll be fun.”

Isabell’s cheeks colored. “R-Really?”

“He means it,” Luk said, already grinning. “He’s not good at sounding excited.”

“I am excited,” Kura protested.

Spy appeared in the doorway as if summoned by the word *trip*. “District 9? I know those highlands. I’ve mapped every trail, every service road, and at least three secret passages beneath the geothermal station.”

Sammy, behind him, folded her arms. “You’ve never been to District 9.”

“I know people who’ve been.”

“You got lost in District 2 last month.”

“That was a deliberate survey.”

Slow stepped around the two of them, Honey-Beam’s repaired arm tucked under his own. “There’s an old western station up there,” he said. “I saw it in a maintenance manual. The underground access was sealed.”

Spy pointed at him. “Exactly. That’s one of the secret passages I was talking about.”

Slow blinked. “I didn’t say secret.”

“Same thing.”

Kura laughed. “I’ll keep an eye out for your legendary map.”

“You’ll be glad you did,” Spy said, solemnly. “When the mountain needs a guide, you’ll remember Spy.”

Sammy dragged him back out by the collar. “He’ll remember the way to the door.”

Slow lingered a moment. His eyes went from Luk to Isabell to Kura. “Sounds nice.”

“Next time,” Kura said. “We’ll bring you along.”

Slow nodded, as if he would hold him to it, then followed Sammy and Spy into the street.

## 2. Mountain Springs Haven

![Inn at District 9](/images/lore/arc_030/inn_1.jpg)

The climb into District 9 took most of the day. The city’s dense towers gave way to wooded slopes and steep roads that curled around the mountain. By the time Kura, Luk, and Isabell reached Mountain Springs Haven, the sky had turned deep blue and the first stars were showing between the pines.

The inn was small, built of dark cedar and warm windowlight, with a narrow stream running beside the entrance. Lili Cole stood on the porch waiting for them, her silver hair pinned back and a shawl around her shoulders.

“You made it before the mountain swallowed the road,” she said, smiling. “Come in, children. Luk’s parents called ahead. Dinner’s ready, but first you look like you could use a soak.”

The three AniBots settled in the sheltered courtyard, where charging sockets had been arranged beneath the eaves. Kabuto-Horn immediately challenged Zerdata to a test of grip strength. Zerdata accepted, then asked if the test had rules.

![AniBots chargers at the inn](/images/lore/arc_030/inn_2.jpg)

“The rule is: don’t lose,” Kabuto-Horn said.

Swan-Grace folded its wings with a patient rustle. “That is not a rule. That is a wish.”

Kura and Luk laughed as Isabell watched from the doorway. It felt good to hear their AniBots talking without the pressure of a battle between them.

The natural baths were separated by a tall bamboo partition. Kura and Luk eased into the steaming water on one side, while Isabell settled in the women’s bath next door. The heat loosened the tension in Kura’s shoulders. For a while they talked about harmless things: the winding roads, Luk’s ongoing claim that Kabuto-Horn could beat any bot in a straight charge, and the rumors that had followed Kura since the Trouble Crew fight.

![Thermal watter](/images/lore/arc_030/inn_3.jpg)

Then Luk’s voice changed.

“So... Isabell?”

“What about her?”

“You know.”

“Know what?”

“Seriously? You haven’t figured it out?”

“Figured what out?”

Luk let out a groan. “She likes you.”

On the other side of the bamboo, Isabell froze. Heat climbed into her face.

“What? No way,” Kura said. “Me?”

“Who else would I mean?”

“I thought she was just being friendly.”

“You can rebuild a damaged memory bus, but you can’t recognize a crush from three feet away.”

Isabell pressed both hands to her face. *Big brother! Why would you tell him that?*

“Well,” Kura said slowly, “she’s really nice. I like spending time with her.”

Isabell’s heart gave a quick, hopeful beat.

“Oh?” Luk said. “Interesting.”

“That’s not what I meant.”

The conversation stumbled onward, but Kura couldn’t quite stop thinking about what Luk had said. On the other side of the partition, Isabell tried to pretend she had not heard a word.

Dinner was served in the inn’s dining room: rice, grilled river fish, mountain greens, and a fragrant broth made from local herbs. Lili listened as the three friends shared stories. Luk talked about the World Cup finals and his dream of meeting Kura there. Isabell spoke about school and how she wanted to become a Handler who could help people feel safe around AniBots. Kura told Lili about the workshop and how Zerdata had come to him from his father.

Lili’s smile softened. “You’re both carrying something someone else began.”

“I suppose,” Kura said.

“My husband and I built this place together,” Lili said. “He died ten years ago. Before he did, he asked me to keep living happily and to keep welcoming people through the door. I’ve tried to do both.”

Kura thought of the old notebook he had found in his father’s office and of the years when he had been afraid to open that door. He understood the feeling of being left with something that was both a gift and a question.

Lili poured them more tea. “There’s plenty of life in what we inherit. We just have to decide what to do with it.”

After dinner, they sat on the porch and listened to the forest. Beyond the trees, a faint metallic clang rang out from somewhere below the ridge.

Lili glanced toward the sound. “That’s the third time this week.”

“What is?” Kura asked.

“Someone’s been breaking into the old storage sheds. Equipment has gone missing. A couple of monitoring units were damaged near the geothermal station, too. At first we thought it was an animal or a maintenance bot gone wrong, but the marks are too clean.”

“You reported it?” Luk asked.

“The local office knows. They’re short-handed, and the trouble has mostly been at night. I’d look into it myself, but the mountain trails aren’t as easy as they used to be.”

Kura and Luk exchanged a glance.

“We can take a look in the morning,” Kura said.

“Only if you promise not to go charging off after every noise,” Lili replied.

Luk grinned. “I can promise for Kura.”

“You can’t,” Isabell said.

The courtyard AniBots had heard the clang, too. Zerdata’s ears tilted toward the mountain. Its optics pulsed once, then returned to a relaxed glow.

## 3. A Familiar Rival

The next morning, the village looked as though it had been painted into the mountainside. Cedar houses and narrow stone lanes followed the slope, and the steam from distant thermal vents drifted in pale ribbons through the trees.

The trio walked past a row of damaged monitoring posts. One had been pulled from its mount; another had a neatly cut cable. Kura crouched to inspect the severed line.

“That’s not an animal,” he said. “The cut’s too straight.”

“And this bolt was removed with a powered driver,” Luk added. “They weren’t just smashing things.”

“Someone’s looking for parts,” Isabell said.

“Or a way into somewhere,” Kura replied.

“A surprisingly reasonable deduction,” said a familiar voice behind them.

Anda Tal stood at the edge of the path, immaculate as ever despite the mountain dust. Ancient-Wyrm waited beside him, its dark frame still and watchful.

“Anda,” Luk said. “What are you doing here?”

“Taking a break from people who ask obvious questions.” Anda looked at Zerdata. “I heard the rumors about your old machine. They seem to have grown more impressive with distance.”

“And you’re still as welcoming as ever,” Kura said.

“I’m not here for your holiday.” Anda pointed to the damaged cable. “I’m here because the geothermal monitoring network has registered irregular drops in pressure and power. Every incident is centered around infrastructure older than the current district grid.”

Kura stood. “You think somebody’s stealing from the substation.”

“I think someone is searching for access to the underground maintenance section. The break-ins are only a means to an end.”

Anda’s gaze settled briefly on Zerdata. “An old system might interest you.”

“Maybe,” Kura said. “But if they’re damaging the station, we should stop them.”

“That is the first useful thing you’ve said.”

“I was going to say the same about you.”

Luk stepped between them before the argument could warm up. “We’ll check the western trail. You can come along, Anda. Try to keep up.”

“I was about to suggest the same.”

They spent the afternoon following the trail toward the western geothermal station. The old access road had partially collapsed, forcing them onto a narrow track through pines and exposed rock. At several points, they found fresh boot prints and deep tracks made by heavy AniBots.

Zerdata paused near a split in the trail, ear dishes turning as it listened. “Metal vibration. Faint. Below us.”

Kura leaned over the edge. A drainage channel ran beneath the path, mostly hidden by brush. Scraped stone and a fresh boot print marked its lip.

“Spy would call that one of his secret passages,” Luk said.

“For once,” Kura answered, “he might be right about the passage.”

The channel led to a service gate built into the hillside. Its lock had been pried at, but not opened. Anda examined the mechanism without touching it.

“They tried brute force,” he said. “This access was sealed decades ago. They’ll need a power cutter to get through.”

“Then they haven’t made it in yet,” Isabell said.

“Not yet,” Anda agreed.

From somewhere beyond the gate came a low mechanical thump. All four AniBots shifted into alert stances. But the sound did not repeat. The group decided to report what they had found and return before nightfall.

On the way back, Luk kept looking at Kura and Isabell with an expression that made Kura suspicious.

“What?” Kura asked.

“Nothing.”

“You’re doing the face.”

“What face?”

“The face you make when you know something I don’t.”

Isabell nearly tripped over a root. “Can we talk about something else?”

Anda walked ahead of them, pretending not to hear.

## 4. The Second Night

Their second day was almost peaceful. They explored the village, ate lunch at a tiny noodle stand, and let the AniBots test their balance on a broad, flat clearing. Kabuto-Horn charged a fallen log, bounced off, and insisted it had been a tactical test. Swan-Grace performed a graceful series of leaps over the same log. Zerdata tried to imitate it and landed with a clank.

Even Anda stayed with them longer than he had meant to. Ancient-Wyrm and Zerdata traded observations about the forest’s soundscape, while Kabuto-Horn and Swan-Grace argued over whether a sprint or a jump was the better way to cross rough ground.

That night, the inn was quiet. Rain tapped lightly on the roof. Kura was almost asleep when the mountain erupted.

A deafening blast rolled through the valley. The windows trembled in their frames. Orange light flashed beyond the western ridge, then the village lights flickered and went dark.

Lili hurried into the hall with a lantern. “What was that?”

Zerdata’s ears snapped toward the mountains. “Explosion detected near the western geothermal station. Structural vibration continues.”

Luk was already pulling on his jacket. “So much for a relaxing holiday.”

“The local lines are down,” Anda said, checking his compact monitor. “If the station’s pressure valves were hit, the damage could spread.”

“We have to get the villagers away from the road,” Isabell said.

They moved quickly. Lili began knocking on doors while Isabell and Swan-Grace guided families toward the stone shelter near the village square. Luk and Kabuto-Horn cleared a fallen signpost and helped two elders through the dark. Anda sent Ancient-Wyrm to scout the road ahead.

Kura followed Zerdata toward the western end of the village, where two unfamiliar AniBots were tearing at a utility cabinet. One was compact and quick, with cutting arms; the other carried a heavy shield plate bolted across its chest.

“They’re the ones from the station,” Kura said.

The quick AniBot lunged at Zerdata. It ducked under the first slash, twisted, and drove a shoulder into the attacker’s side. The machine skidded across the road, its cutting arm carving a bright line in the stones. The armored bot charged while Zerdata was recovering.

“Right!” Kura called.

Zerdata hopped aside. The armored bot smashed into the cabinet instead, sending sparks into the rain. Kura saw a third machine turn toward the shelter, where Isabell was still guiding villagers.

“Swan-Grace, above!” Isabell called.

Swan-Grace sprang upward, wings opening. It swept down with a precise kick that knocked the pursuing bot off balance, then landed between it and the shelter. Another scavenger AniBot came from the opposite side, pinning Swan-Grace between them.

“Swan!”

The first attacker swung. Swan-Grace leaned back, the blow passing inches from its visor, and answered with a sharp rapier thrust to the machine’s shoulder joint. The second bot caught its wing and yanked. Swan-Grace hit the ground hard.

Isabell moved to help, but two more scavengers cut off the lane. They closed in, forcing her back against the stone wall.

“W-What do I do?” she whispered.

Across the road, Kura saw her trapped.

“Zerdata! With me!”

Zerdata turned away from the armored bot and sprinted toward Swan-Grace. It slipped between the two attackers, struck one in the knee joint, then hooked an arm under Swan-Grace’s and pulled it clear of the second’s grip.

Kura ran straight for Isabell. He reached her just as one of the scavengers raised its arm. Kura stepped in front of her, arms out, planting himself between Isabell and the machine.

“Stay behind me.”

His voice held no shake, no embarrassment. Isabell stared at him, caught for half a breath by the fierce certainty in his face.

“Kura...”

Zerdata and Swan-Grace moved together. Swan-Grace feinted left; Zerdata ducked under the enemy’s swing and drove its shoulder into the bot’s center. The attacker crashed into the one blocking the lane. Both stumbled aside.

“Now!” Isabell shouted to the villagers.

The last families hurried into the shelter. Luk and Kabuto-Horn arrived, knocking the armored bot away from the utility cabinet with a low, sweeping charge. Anda’s Ancient-Wyrm swept in from the dark and pinned the quick attacker’s cutting arm to the road.

The scavenger AniBots retreated toward the station, drawing the group after them. Kura looked toward the shelter.

“Isabell, stay with the villagers.”

“I will. Be careful.”

Her eyes met his for a moment. Then the others ran toward the ridge.

## 5. Three Leaders

![Station](/images/lore/arc_030/inn_4.jpg)

The western station stood half-lit against the mountainside. One service structure had been damaged by the blast, and a cloud of steam hissed from a broken pipe. The explosion had not destroyed the station, but the vibration of its pumps sounded uneven. If the scavengers cut through the wrong conduit, the damage could worsen.

Three figures waited in the loading yard, each beside a more heavily reinforced AniBot than the ones in the village. Behind them, a fourth scavenger was trying to open the service gate with a cutter.

“You came all this way to steal a box of old relays?” Luk called.

The broadest leader stepped forward. “Those components are worth more than your whole district. Move aside.”

“No,” Kura said.

The leader gave a small signal. The three AniBots attacked.

The first one was a hulking, armored machine with reinforced shoulders and a pair of heavy forearm guards. It charged Kabuto-Horn head-on.

“That one’s mine,” Luk said.

Kabuto-Horn met the charge with both feet braced. The impact drove it backward, its heels scoring furrows in the gravel. The armored opponent slammed down its fists, trying to pin Kabuto-Horn under its weight.

“Roll!” Luk shouted.

Kabuto-Horn dropped low and twisted. The enemy’s fists struck empty ground. Kabuto-Horn hooked a horn under the armor’s center plate and flipped. The big machine crashed onto its back, then rolled before Kabuto-Horn could follow through.

The rival slammed into Kabuto-Horn’s side. Both bots went down. Luk winced, but Kabuto-Horn pushed up, dug in, and caught the next punch on its forearms.

“Don’t stop it,” Luk called. “Turn it!”

Kabuto-Horn let the blow slide along its guard, caught the attacker’s wrist, and pivoted. Its opponent’s own momentum carried it past. Kabuto-Horn rammed its shoulder into the back of the knee joint. The armored machine toppled forward. This time Kabuto-Horn pinned it with one heavy boot against its chest.

“Done,” Luk said. “Stay down.”

On the far side of the yard, Anda faced the second leader. Their AniBot was slender and precise, surrounded by small disc-shaped traps that skittered over the ground and flashed when Ancient-Wyrm approached.

“You’ll want to watch your footing,” Anda said, as if stating a fact rather than warning anyone.

Ancient-Wyrm’s tail snapped around one disc and flung it into the air. The disc burst in a brief flash. The enemy fired two more from its wrists. Ancient-Wyrm ducked behind a steel support, but the discs curved around the post.

Anda watched their paths. “It’s steering them with magnetic pulses. The nearest one always turns toward metal.”

“Can Ancient-Wyrm use that?” Kura shouted over the fight.

“It can use anything I give it.”

Anda nodded once. Ancient-Wyrm drove its claws into the gravel, then ripped up a strip of metal mesh left from the damaged fence. It held the mesh out. The discs snapped toward it and clung there, sparking harmlessly.

Ancient-Wyrm whipped the mesh aside and rushed in. The enemy tried to retreat, but its feet caught on the fallen strip. Ancient-Wyrm struck the bot’s weapon arm, then its shoulder, then swept its legs from beneath it. The strategist’s machine hit the ground and stayed there.

“Your timing was adequate,” Anda said to Kura as he passed.

“That’s the nicest thing you’ve ever said to me.”

“Don’t get used to it.”

Kura faced the third leader. The commander’s AniBot was built for speed as much as power, with reinforced limbs and a bright energy unit mounted along its back. Its movements were crisp and economical. Zerdata held its ground beside Kura, but its posture shifted to protect one side where old repairs still showed.

“That one’s stronger than the others,” Kura said.

“Confirmed,” Zerdata replied. “Output exceeds safe sustained parameters.”

“Then we don’t let it set the pace.”

The commander’s machine burst forward. Zerdata sidestepped the first strike, but the bot’s second swing caught its shoulder and sent it into a stack of crates. Wood splintered. Zerdata rolled clear as a kick smashed the place where its head had been.

Kura watched the opponent’s feet, listening to the shifts in its servos. “It loads its right leg before each rush. Zerdata, wait for it.”

The commander charged again. Zerdata stood still until the last moment, then dipped aside. Its opponent’s fist struck a steel post. Zerdata drove an elbow into the exposed side panel and sprang away before the return strike could land.

The commander twisted and clipped Zerdata across the back. The older machine stumbled. Its cyan optics flickered.

“Zerdata!”

“Still operational.”

The leader laughed. “That relic can barely stand.”

Kura’s jaw tightened. He looked past the fighters. The fourth scavenger was almost through the gate, and steam was beginning to thicken around the broken pipe. The station was becoming less stable by the minute.

“Zerdata, make it turn. I’ll get the gate.”

“Understood.”

Kura sprinted for the service panel while Zerdata darted in and out of the commander’s reach. It evaded a punch, ducked under a backhand, then struck the bot’s knee. The commander stumbled but caught Zerdata by the arm and flung it across the yard.

Kura reached the panel and saw the cutter had burned through the outer cover. He pulled the emergency isolation lever down. The hissing pipe fell quiet as the damaged line shut off.

“The gate’s isolated!” he shouted. “Zerdata, now!”

The commander charged. Zerdata’s ears turned toward the machine, tracking the changing pitch of the servos. At the last instant, it dropped low, letting the charge carry the enemy over its back. Zerdata caught the reinforced ankle and turned with the machine’s momentum. The commander hit the gravel on its side.

Zerdata climbed over it, but its arms shook with the effort. The machine raised one elbow and knocked Zerdata off. Kura saw the energy unit on its back pulse brighter—the same instant each burst of speed began.

“The pack! Strike the mount!”

Zerdata rolled away from another punch, sprang back in, and hit the exposed mounting bracket with both hands. Metal buckled. The energy unit flickered. The commander’s next rush faltered, its legs jerking as its output dropped.

Zerdata stepped aside and swept its foot through the bot’s stance. The commander crashed down. Zerdata planted one knee across its back and held it still.

“Don’t make me break it,” Kura said to the leader.

The leader looked around the yard. His three AniBots were down, and the station workers had begun arriving with the local security team. He raised both hands.

“We came for the pre-standard relays,” he said. “The underground section was supposed to be sealed. We didn’t know the pressure line was that close.”

“You knew you were cutting into a live station,” Anda said.

The leader had no answer.

The security team took the scavengers into custody. Kura helped a station worker check the isolation controls while Luk and Anda made sure the AniBots could be safely powered down. Zerdata stood a little apart, one shoulder sagging.

“You’re damaged,” Kura said quietly.

“Wear is significant. I can continue standing.”

“That’s not the same as being fine.”

“I am aware.”

Kura placed a hand on Zerdata’s arm. “We’ll get you fixed properly when we’re home.”

## 6. Sunrise Over District 9

By sunrise, the danger had passed. The village lights came back on one street at a time. Residents began clearing debris and checking the damaged equipment. The geothermal station remained under guard while its crew assessed the broken lines.

Lili met them outside the inn with a basket of breakfast wrapped in cloth. “You came for a holiday and gave us a night we won’t forget.”

“Sorry about the trouble,” Kura said.

“You didn’t cause it.” She squeezed his shoulder. “You helped us through it.”

Anda stood nearby with Ancient-Wyrm. “Your decisions were good, Kura. But that chassis won’t keep up forever.”

“I know.”

“Then do something about it.”

“I will.”

It was not praise, exactly, but Kura accepted it as the closest thing Anda offered.

Over breakfast, Lili told them they were welcome back whenever they wanted. Luk promised they would return, then immediately began arguing with Kabuto-Horn about who had won their grip contest in the courtyard.

As they packed, Isabell carried a bag beside Kura.

“Are you okay after last night?” he asked.

“Y-Yes. I’m fine. You and Zerdata were amazing.”

“You and Swan-Grace got everyone to safety. That mattered more.”

“Thank you, Kura.”

He smiled and turned to check Zerdata’s damaged shoulder. Luk appeared at Isabell’s side with a suspicious grin.

“Sooo... you were saved by Kura.”

“Big brother!”

“He ran straight to you. Very heroic.”

“Stop it!”

“And that look on your face when he said, ‘Stay behind me’—”

“Luccas!”

Luk burst out laughing. A few steps away, Kura glanced back.

“Everything okay?”

“Yes!” Isabell said.

“Everything’s perfect,” Luk said.

On the road out of the village, Zerdata rode carefully in the transport’s secured rear seat. Its damaged shoulder was braced, and its optics glowed at half brightness. The mountain peaks caught the morning sun, and steam rose in pale threads among the trees.

Kura looked back once at the inn, then at his friends. He had come for a short break from the shop. Now he was leaving with a damaged partner and questions about old components, sealed passages, and machines built with methods he still did not understand.

Back in District 4, he would need better tools and better knowledge to restore Zerdata. Raya knew someone who worked with unusual salvage and old AniBot parts. His name was Master Jax.

![Backing home](/images/lore/arc_030/inn_5.jpg)

For now, the road descended toward the city. Kura checked the shoulder brace once more, then settled beside Zerdata for the journey. Behind them, the highlands grew quiet again.
