## Part I - The World Cup Begins

The Aegis High-Spire Grand Dome rose above District 5 like a polished blade. Its outer walls curved into the sky, reflecting the green canopies and transit lines that circled the sports quarter. Banners for the AniBots World Cup streamed from the entrance towers. Every screen showed the same emblem: two opposing AniBots facing across a bright arena line.

Kura had seen the Dome on broadcasts for years. Standing beneath it with Zerdata beside him, he found it difficult to believe he had made it here.

His rebuilt partner looked different from the machine that had faced Soren in the qualifiers. The old frame’s awkward joints and exposed couplings were gone, replaced with a compact, balanced chassis that moved with quiet precision. The orange armor and familiar foxlike silhouette remained, as did the broad acoustic ears, pale armor panels, and cyan optics. The V2 was newer, stronger, and ready for the biggest stage Kura had ever seen.

“Your heart rate is elevated,” Zerdata said through the earpiece.

“I know.”

“Would you like me to count your breaths?”

“No. I’d like you to pretend not to notice.”

“I can attempt that.”

Luk laughed as Kabuto-Horn rolled up beside them. “It’s a World Cup, Kura. If your heart isn’t trying to escape, you’re not taking it seriously.”

“Yours is doing that?”

“My heart is perfectly calm.” Luk tapped his chest. “It’s the rest of me that’s excited.”

Anda arrived from the registration hall with Ancient-Wyrm behind him. His silver hair was tucked beneath his cap, and he had already scanned the competitor list twice.

“The bracket is randomized,” he said. “No seeding advantage after the opening draw. We could face anyone.”

“Good,” Luk said. “That means everyone has to deal with us.”

“It means you could meet Soren before the final.”

Luk’s grin sharpened. “Then I’ll deal with him first.”

A cheer surged from the east entrance. Kura turned and saw Sammy at the front of a small District 4 crowd. She had a hand-painted banner stretched between herself and Slow. It read **DISTRICT 4 BUILT DIFFERENT**, with the final word written so large that it covered the corner of Sammy’s smiley-face badge.

Spy held a portable speaker over his head. “Make way! Official District 4 support section coming through!”

“You’re not official,” Sammy said.

“I made a list.”

“You made a list of people who owe you snacks.”

“That is a kind of support network.”

Slow walked steadily behind them, carrying a folded sign that had been painted on the back of a repair-shop panel. It showed Zerdata, Kabuto-Horn, and Ancient-Wyrm in rough but recognizable shapes. The younger handlers from the district followed, waving small red-and-cyan pennants.

Sammy stopped in front of Kura. “Don’t get used to this. I’m here for District 4.”

“Of course,” Kura said.

“And if you lose, I’m blaming the repairs.”

“Those were your repairs?” Luk asked.

“No. That’s why I can blame them.”

Spy leaned in. “I’ve got a reliable report that the first opponent is using a secret banned upgrade.”

“Who told you?” Kura asked.

“I’m still looking into that.”

Slow lowered the painted sign. “I think he means the opponent has an upgraded arm.”

Spy looked impressed. “That’s probably it.”

Isabell joined them with Swan-Grace at her side. She had been supporting Kura since the qualifiers, and that morning was no different: she checked Zerdata’s field telemetry, reminded Kura where the team entrance was, then wished him luck as if the entire Dome had narrowed to the three of them.

“Good luck, Kura,” she said. “You’ve prepared for this.”

“Thanks, Isa.”

She turned to Luk. “And you too, big brother. Don’t rush your openings just because you’re excited.”

Luk raised both hands. “I never rush.”

“You rushed through registration and gave them the wrong date of birth.”

“That was a clerical warm-up.”

“Good luck,” she repeated, smiling. “Both of you.”

Luk leaned toward her while Kura checked Zerdata’s wrist actuator. “When will you tell him?”

Isabell’s smile vanished. Her cheeks went pink. “Tell who what?”

“You know exactly what.”

“I’m telling both of you to win your matches.” She nudged his shoulder away. “Go warm up.”

Luk laughed and rolled toward the practice bay. Kura glanced up. “What was that about?”

“Nothing,” Isabell said quickly. “Just brother things.”

Kura accepted the explanation and returned to Zerdata’s diagnostics. Isabell watched him for a moment, then followed Luk toward the team section.

The World Cup opened with a parade of qualified Handlers from across the metropolises. When the announcer called the District 4 contenders, Sammy, Spy, and Slow led the cheers. They stood and shouted for every local competitor, including Kura, Luk, and Anda, even when the district’s other entrants were strangers to them.

Spy’s speaker squealed each time he tried to start a chant. Slow kept the banner from folding over itself. Sammy shouted loud enough to be heard from the upper tiers.

“District Four! District Four!”

“Your chant has only two words,” Spy said.

“It works,” Sammy snapped.

The opening draw assigned each Handler a place in the elimination bracket. Kura, Luk, Anda, and Soren landed on different paths through the first rounds. Soren’s name appeared in the upper half beside Luk’s. Kura’s route ran through the lower half. Anda’s first match was against a disciplined academy Handler from the coastal circuit.

Kura’s first opponent used a long-legged striker built to control distance. The machine drove a staff-like arm toward Zerdata’s chest. Zerdata tilted aside, listening to the actuator wind up before each thrust. Kura called for a short retreat, then sent Zerdata forward just as the opponent committed to a second strike. The V2’s new leg assembly absorbed the landing cleanly. Zerdata tapped the opponent’s weapon joint with its resonant claw and knocked the staff arm off line.

The opponent tried to reset. Zerdata’s acoustic sensors caught the uneven return cycle. Kura spotted the same opening.

“Left side, now!”

Zerdata crossed in, hooked the striker’s ankle, and turned its momentum toward the padded boundary. The opponent landed on one knee and could not rise before the referee’s count ended.

The win felt different from the qualifiers. Zerdata had taken hits without losing its balance. Its frame moved as Kura had imagined it on the workbench, only faster, amid the light and noise of a real arena.

In the stands, Isabell clapped with both hands above her head. Sammy and the others were on their feet, shouting. Luk waved at Kura from the passageway before he and Kabuto-Horn were called to their own match.

The first round lasted two days. Luk won with a compact defense, using Kabuto-Horn’s weight to turn a faster opponent’s charge into a controlled flip. Anda won by reading his opponent’s feints and refusing to follow them. Soren advanced with Tigris-Fury in a match so quick that the broadcast replay slowed the final exchange three times.

As the bracket narrowed, the Dome became louder and the space between matches shorter. Kura studied every recording he could find. He did not spend his time watching only Soren. He watched the other competitors too: their footwork, response patterns, preferred openings, and the ways they changed when their first plan failed.

Between matches, Isabell kept returning to Kura’s side. She checked Zerdata’s readings, offered him water, and reminded him to loosen his shoulders. When Luk came out of a match, she hurried to him as well, inspecting the scrape on Kabuto-Horn’s guard and telling him he had done well.

“You say that to him too,” Luk said once, “but you look much more worried when Kura’s fighting.”

“I worry about both of you.”

“Not equally.”

“Go find something to eat.”

The quarterfinals began under a ring of white lights. Anda’s match was first. His opponent used a defensive frame with shifting panels that redirected impacts rather than meeting them head-on. Anda anticipated the first pattern and had Ancient-Wyrm attack the exposed hip joint. The opposing Handler changed tactics mid-match, folding the panels inward to bait a second strike and then turning the trap into a low sweep.

Ancient-Wyrm recovered, but the timing had slipped. Anda recalculated, tried to force the opponent back toward the boundary, and left its flank open for a counter. A precision strike hit the dragon frame’s shoulder assembly. Ancient-Wyrm dropped to one knee. The referee called the match before the damage could worsen.

Anda stood still for a moment, then accepted the result with a short nod. He returned to the team area beside Kura and Luk. His face was composed, but his fingers kept turning the edge of his glove.

“You were ahead until they changed their pattern,” Kura said.

“I was ahead until I assumed they would keep following the pattern.” Anda glanced toward the arena. “A correct prediction is useless if I stop observing.”

Sammy called from the stands, “You were still the best one out there!”

Spy lifted his speaker. “I have the official results and an unofficial appeal form!”

“Put it away,” Anda said, though his mouth twitched into a brief smile.

Kura won his quarterfinal later that afternoon. The opponent came in with a fast pair of blades, making Zerdata give ground through the first half of the match. Kura waited for the left blade to cross over the right, then had Zerdata pivot inside the arc and strike the opponent’s hip actuator. The machine stumbled. Zerdata’s second hit disarmed one blade, and the referee ended the match after the opponent could not continue safely.

Luk’s quarterfinal was a harder contest. Kabuto-Horn took a direct hit to the shoulder guard and was pushed close to the boundary, but Luk held his ground and used the frame’s leverage to turn the next rush. He won by flipping the opponent onto its back and pinning its arm until the referee called it.

In the stands, Isabell cheered for him just as loudly as she had for Kura. She waved her rabbit charm and called his name until Luk looked up and saluted with one arm.

The semifinal match between Luk and Soren was scheduled for the next morning.

Luk met Kura in the preparation corridor before check-in. “I’ve watched his last three matches. He’s faster than he was at the qualifiers.”

“Then don’t give him the same openings,” Kura said.

“I won’t.” Luk looked down at Kabuto-Horn. “If I can get him to commit to a straight charge, I can stop him.”

“You can,” Kura said.

Luk grinned. “That sounded almost confident.”

“It was confident.”

“Good. Keep doing that.”

The arena filled early. Sammy’s group had made a fresh sign for Luk, with **HOLD THE LINE, LUK!** painted across it. Spy had added a small note in the corner: *Soren’s tail has a weak spot*. Slow had crossed that out and written: *Ask before touching the tail.*

Tigris-Fury entered in silence, its orange armor catching the overhead lights. Kabuto-Horn rolled onto the field with its guard raised. Luk bowed to the referee, then glanced toward Isabell. She gave him both thumbs up.

The match began with Soren testing the guard. Tigris-Fury darted in, struck twice, and sprang back before Kabuto-Horn could lock its stance. Luk did not chase. He kept Kabuto-Horn planted near center and made Soren come to him.

On the fourth pass, Tigris-Fury attacked low. Kabuto-Horn caught the blow across its reinforced guard, then drove forward with its horn. Tigris-Fury twisted away, but the impact grazed its side and forced Soren to reset.

The crowd roared. District 4’s section roared louder.

Luk saw an opening and committed. Kabuto-Horn surged forward, lifting its guard to hook Tigris-Fury’s shoulder. Soren had been waiting for the move. Tigris-Fury dropped beneath the hook, pivoted around Kabuto-Horn’s planted tread, and struck the exposed rear leg. Kabuto-Horn dropped to one knee. Luk called for it to turn, but Tigris-Fury struck the guard hinge before it could recover.

Luk kept the fight going. Kabuto-Horn used its good side to fend off another rush and nearly caught Tigris-Fury in a second flip. Soren pulled the machine clear at the last instant. The next exchange ended with Kabuto-Horn’s front guard pinned against the floor and one tread locked under Tigris-Fury’s foot.

The referee counted. Luk called for a reset, but Kabuto-Horn’s actuator could not free itself. The match was over.

Soren stepped back. Tigris-Fury remained poised beside him, barely scuffed.

Luk came off the field with Kabuto-Horn leaning against his shoulder. Isabell reached them first. “Are you hurt?”

“Only my pride,” he said. “Kabuto-Horn’s going to need a new hinge.”

She checked the frame anyway, then hugged him briefly. Luk gave her a quick squeeze in return.

Kura stood nearby, looking at the semifinal board. Luk’s expression changed when he saw him.

“Will you win, right?” Luk asked.

Kura took a breath. “I’ll give it everything I have.”

“Good. That’s what I wanted to hear.”

Anda came up behind them. He had recovered enough of his usual sharpness to cross his arms and say, “Try to not get destroyed this time!”

Kura looked at him. “That’s your encouragement?”

“It’s a clear objective. Avoid the exact failure mode from the qualifiers.”

“Very motivating,” Luk said.

“Then it should be easy to remember.”

Isabell shook her head, though she was smiling. “Both of you are impossible.”

The other semifinal took place that afternoon. Kura faced a Handler whose machine combined shielded forearms with a short-range pulse driver. The opponent controlled the center and forced Zerdata to work around the edges. Kura watched the pulse driver’s recharge cycle and used Zerdata’s acoustic reading to time a narrow opening between blasts. Zerdata disarmed the driver, then pushed the shielded frame across the line with two measured strikes.

Kura had reached the final.

The championship match was set: Kura Ato and Zerdata V2 against Soren Vance and Tigris-Fury.

That evening, the Grand Dome’s screens displayed the two finalists side by side. Sammy, Spy, and Slow brought their District 4 banner to the front rows. Isabell stayed with Kura until the Handler access doors closed, then took her place beside Luk and Anda.

“You’re going to be okay,” she told him.

“I know.”

“You don’t have to answer right away,” Luk said as the doors began to close. “But maybe after you win, you can talk to her.”

Isabell’s eyes widened. “Luk!”

“What? I’m talking about the match.”

Kura looked from one to the other. Luk gave him an innocent shrug. The doors closed before Kura could ask.

## Part II - The Championship Final

The Grand Dome was full when the championship match began. The floor had been reset and inspected. Energy barriers shimmered along the perimeter, and the central display showed the World Cup crest above the names of both Handlers.

Kura entered with Zerdata at his side. Across the field, Soren walked with Tigris-Fury. The Primal AniBot looked as controlled and fast as it had during the qualifiers. Zerdata’s new chassis was sturdier, more balanced, but still smaller and lighter.

Soren studied the rebuilt frame. “You’ve changed the body.”

“I rebuilt it.”

“Doesn’t change what happened last time.”

“No,” Kura said. “It changes what happens now.”

The referee raised a hand. The crowd fell quiet.

“Handlers ready?”

Kura set his stance. “Ready.”

Soren nodded once.

The signal sounded.

Tigris-Fury moved first. It crossed the field in a blur, claws sweeping toward Zerdata’s chest. Kura called a sidestep. Zerdata moved just outside the strike, but the follow-up clipped its shoulder armor and sent it skidding across the polished floor.

Soren did not let up. Tigris-Fury pressed with a sequence of fast attacks, forcing Zerdata to retreat. Kura’s hand tightened around the control grip. He had watched the recordings, but seeing the speed in person made every strike feel closer than expected.

“Zerdata, read the rhythm.”

“I am listening.”

Tigris-Fury’s left claw cut high, then its right leg snapped forward. Zerdata heard the motor pitch rise before the kick and turned its torso. The strike scraped along its side instead of landing squarely. Zerdata used the contact to spin behind Tigris-Fury and hit its back plate with the resonant claw.

The strike made Tigris-Fury stumble one step.

Soren’s eyes narrowed.

Kura had not stopped the attack. He had redirected its force and made it useful. The new frame’s actuators held. Zerdata returned to center before Tigris-Fury could turn.

The match settled into a fast exchange. Tigris-Fury kept trying to corner Zerdata and attack from its blind side. Zerdata used acoustic readings to anticipate the turns and slipped away at the last moment. Twice, it answered with a short pulse that made Tigris-Fury’s footwork falter. The crowd rose each time the machines met.

“You’ve improved,” Soren called across the floor. “I’ll give you that.”

“Thanks.”

“But a few new parts won’t make you a champion.”

Tigris-Fury feinted low, then sprang up with a claw strike. Zerdata blocked it with the forearm guard. The impact rang through the arena. Kura felt the vibration in the controller and heard Zerdata’s frame absorb it.

“Back two steps!”

Zerdata obeyed. Tigris-Fury’s next strike met empty space. Zerdata pivoted, drove a short burst into Tigris-Fury’s elbow joint, and stepped clear before Soren could retaliate.

Soren shifted tactics, using Tigris-Fury’s speed to close the gaps before Zerdata could build a counter. The Primal AniBot’s strikes grew heavier. One hit caught Zerdata’s left side and forced it onto a knee. The V2 frame stayed intact, but a warning flashed on Kura’s display.

“Left hip load rising,” Kura said. “Keep your weight centered.”

“Understood.”

Zerdata rose and turned away from Tigris-Fury’s next charge. It angled toward the center and forced Tigris-Fury to cross its path. The two machines collided shoulder to shoulder. Zerdata’s reinforced frame held. Tigris-Fury slid away, claws scraping the floor.

The audience erupted.

In the front section, Isabell stood with both hands around her communicator. She cheered for Kura, then glanced toward Luk whenever Kabuto-Horn’s repair status appeared on the recovery monitor. Luk leaned over the rail, shouting tactical suggestions that Kura could barely hear.

“You can’t keep dodging forever,” Soren said.

“I’m not trying to.”

“Then fight.”

Tigris-Fury rushed. Zerdata met it with a rising forearm and a tight counterstrike to its chest. The blow landed cleanly. Tigris-Fury’s torso rocked back. Soren stepped to the side, eyes fixed on Kura.

“You’re proud of this?” he said. “Your father built a relic and left you to pretend it was a legacy.”

Kura’s grip went rigid. For one heartbeat, the arena noise dimmed. He remembered the broken first chassis, the qualifiers, and the way Soren had walked away while Zerdata lay on the floor.

“Leave Ty out of this,” Kura said.

Soren’s smile sharpened. “Why? Is his name all you have left of him?”

Zerdata’s head turned toward Soren. Its ears angled forward, but its feet stayed planted beside Kura. “Ty gave me a body. He gave me a chance to stand beside Kura. You know nothing about what he left us.”

Kura glanced at the diagnostics. Zerdata’s core synchronization had risen, but it was still within range. “Zerdata, focus on Tigris. Ignore him.”

Soren looked toward Tigris-Fury. “It can hear me, can’t it? Does it know what Ty made it for? Or did he leave it unfinished, too?”

“Stop,” Kura warned.

“Your father built an obsolete shell around an Ancient chip and called it a future. You rebuilt the shell, but you still don’t know what’s inside.” Soren lifted a hand. Tigris-Fury paced in a slow arc around Zerdata. “What kind of legacy is that?”

Tigris-Fury’s visor brightened. A speaker in its chest crackled, then produced a low, synthetic voice. “Your ears hear every motor, fox. Can they hear your Handler failing you?”

Zerdata’s fists tightened. It did not turn toward Soren. Its attention shifted to Tigris-Fury.

“Do not speak to Kura that way,” Zerdata said.

Tigris-Fury gave a short, mechanical growl. “Then make me stop.”

“Zerdata, hold position,” Kura said. “This is what Soren wants.”

Soren kept pressing. “You can’t even remember your own beginning. You’re just a broken machine carrying someone else’s name.”

Tigris-Fury’s voice came again. “No body. No past. Just a core someone found and bolted into a frame.”

Zerdata’s anger sharpened. “I am not a thing he found.”

Its motor pitch climbed. Kura’s display showed the synchronization level rising through amber. Zerdata’s arms tensed. It stayed locked on Tigris-Fury, waiting for the match to continue.

“Zerdata, listen to me,” Kura said. “Stay with the plan.”

For a moment, it did. Then Tigris-Fury sprang forward and struck Zerdata’s chest with a claw, driving it back two steps.

“Still waiting for him to tell you what you are?” Tigris-Fury taunted.

Something in Zerdata’s control pattern broke.

It surged toward Tigris-Fury. The first blow crashed against its guard. The second drove through the defense and forced Tigris-Fury to stumble.

“Zerdata, disengage!” Kura shouted.

The machine could no longer hear him. It pressed Tigris-Fury across the field with a speed Kura had never seen from the V2 chassis. Its claw struck high, then low. Tigris-Fury blocked one attack and was hit by the next, sent sliding toward the boundary.

Soren’s confident expression disappeared. “Tigris, counter!”

Tigris-Fury tried. Zerdata evaded the counter and hit its shoulder hard enough to twist the frame sideways. It was becoming stronger and faster with every exchange, pushing Tigris-Fury out of its rhythm and taking control of the fight.

Kura’s display flashed amber, then red. Green and yellow light began to bleed through the seams of Zerdata’s orange armor. The glow pulsed from inside the chassis, tracing its shoulders, arms, and torso.

A system warning appeared:

> BERSERK MODE: ACTIVE  
> MEMORY UNLOCK PROTOCOL INITIATED  
> ANCIENT CORE SYNCHRONIZATION ESCALATING  
> WARNING: ENERGY OUTPUT EXCEEDS MONITORING RANGE

“Zerdata!” Kura tried to lower the output limit. The controls rejected the input. “Can you hear me?”

There was no reply. Zerdata drove Tigris-Fury back another step.

## Part III - The Memories Return

Kura’s earpiece filled with a low, repeating tone. His diagnostic interface broke into fragments as previously locked memory sectors changed status from **LOCKED** to **ACTIVE**.

The present did not disappear for Kura. He remained on the arena floor, gripping the control unit and watching Zerdata. He could not enter the memories or communicate with Zerdata inside them. His display rendered only broken images and signal traces while the machine’s body continued fighting without hearing him.

“Zerdata, respond!”

No reply.

Zerdata lowered its center of gravity and set its feet, facing Tigris-Fury. Its fingers flexed around the Resonant Strike Claw. The targeting sensors locked on. The body kept reacting to the battle, but the focus in its optics had gone distant. It looked like Zerdata was trapped in a trance, preparing another attack without seeing the arena in front of it.

Then the images changed.

There was no AniBot body in the memory. Vulpes Zerda existed as a core suspended in a testing cradle, surrounded by sensors, wire arrays, and early communication equipment. It had no arms to raise, no legs to move, no eyes to focus. Researchers were testing new systems that could send signals to the core and translate its responses.

A tone repeated. The core answered with a pulse of its own.

The screen filled with a different waveform. Another Generation 0 core had responded from a neighboring chamber. A strong, direct signal arrived first; another followed as a deep, steady rhythm. Then a more intricate pattern threaded between them. The cores exchanged pulses through the new communication system, learning to recognize one another without bodies or voices.

A researcher changed the signal. Vulpes Zerda answered. The other cores responded again. The pattern became more complex, less like a test and more like a conversation.

Kura saw the traces on his display, but could not hear what the cores meant to each other. He called Zerdata’s name again. Nothing came back.

The flashback shifted to another test: a simulated landscape shown through sensory relays. Vulpes Zerda received a stream of motion, temperature, and sound. The core tried to map the world from signals alone. There was no frame waiting for it, no body to carry it across the landscape—only the new system and the patterns it could learn.

Then the room’s lights went white. The memory fractured into static.

On the arena floor, Zerdata’s right arm drew back. The claw remained aimed at Tigris-Fury’s torso. Its green-yellow glow brightened, and the energy readings climbed beyond the interface’s scale.

Kura took a step forward, but the field boundary held him at the edge. “Stop the match!”

The referee signaled for an emergency pause. The arena system acknowledged the command, but the warning lights around the field flickered as the barrier tried to compensate for Zerdata’s rising output. Tigris-Fury braced itself. Zerdata remained locked on it, silent and motionless except for the tightening of its arm.

Kura’s earpiece crackled. He could not reach Zerdata through their usual link. He could not guide it through the memories or pull it out by calling its name. The diagnostic screen showed only unrecognized files opening one after another.

The memory fragments continued: cores responding across separate chambers; new systems translating pulses into language; a field of virtual sensations built around a mind that had never moved through a body. Then a harsh tone sounded, and every signal cut out at once.

Zerdata’s body shifted its weight onto the forward foot. The arm holding the claw drew farther back, gathering force for a decisive strike against Tigris-Fury.

“Zerdata, please,” Kura said.

There was no answer.

Soren had stopped taunting. His focus was on Tigris-Fury, which had taken several heavy blows and was being forced back. He looked at the referee and the unstable barrier. Tigris-Fury raised its claws to guard its chest. Neither Handler knew what Zerdata would do next.

The referee tried the emergency halt again. The signal tone broke into static.

In the memory, the white testing room flashed again. Vulpes Zerda’s core answered the repeated tone. Across the chambers, the other Generation 0 signals answered too.

On the arena floor, Zerdata’s core pulsed in time with the old pattern. The yellow light edged brighter through the green, and the whole Dome answered with a deep, growing vibration.

## Part IV - Anima Limit Breaker

Anda had both hands on the rail. “That isn’t the crowd,” he said.

Luk felt it too: a low vibration shuddering through the platform beneath their feet. It came in a slow, uneven pulse. The audience was screaming now, but the movement of the building did not match the sound. The Grand Dome itself was vibrating.

The arena lights trembled in their housings. Dust shook loose from a seam high above the field. The safety barrier around the floor brightened as it compensated for Zerdata’s rising energy.

“Isabell, get back from the rail,” Anda said.

She stepped away, still watching Kura. “The barrier’s holding.”

“It should be holding,” Luk said. “That’s not the same as knowing why the building is moving.”

On the field, Zerdata remained poised before Tigris-Fury. Its claw was drawn back. The memory trance had not loosened its hold. Green and yellow radiance wrapped around the orange armor, and energy curled between its raised arms in a bright, unstable sphere.

Kura’s earpiece showed no usable readings. “Zerdata!”

No reply.

Soren looked at Tigris-Fury. The machine’s armor was scuffed and its stance had weakened under Zerdata’s attacks. The arena display showed its stability dropping.

“Final charge!” Soren shouted. “Destroy it before it fires!”

Tigris-Fury accelerated straight toward Zerdata. Its claws angled forward, its legs driving against the floor hard enough to leave bright marks. It tracked the incoming opponent, arms rising as if the core had followed an instinct older than the memories holding it.

The energy sphere swelled between its hands. The light was so bright that the arena screens went white. The building’s vibration climbed into a deep, resonant note.

Tigris-Fury closed the final distance.

Zerdata brought both arms down.

“ANIMA LIMIT BREAKER!”

A vast beam of Anima energy burst across the field. Green and gold light swallowed the space between the two machines. The shockwave lifted dust and fragments from the floor. The protective barrier flared white as it caught the outer edge of the blast.

Tigris-Fury was struck squarely. The force carried it backward across the arena and into the reinforced wall beyond the barrier. Armor panels buckled. One arm went limp, and the frame dropped hard to the floor, its optics dark.

The energy did not simply leave Zerdata. The pulse surged through its chassis and returned along the harmonic channels in a blinding recoil. The wave struck Zerdata from within and drove it several steps backward. Its raised claw dropped. Its joints locked, then released.

The memory tone snapped off.

The testing room vanished from the diagnostic display. The images of the bodiless core and the signals from the other Generation 0 chips collapsed into dark lines. Zerdata’s fingers opened. The green-yellow glow folded inward and disappeared from its armor seams.

Kura’s earpiece crackled once.

“Kura?” Zerdata’s voice came through, strained and confused. “I—where am I?”

“You’re in the arena. You’re with me.” Kura ran to the boundary. “The berserk mode is over. Stay still. I’m here.”

Zerdata lowered its head. “I remember the tests. The signals. I could not hear you.”

“I know. You’re back now.”

The beam cut off completely. Emergency lights came on around the Dome. The barrier remained intact, though its field shimmered unevenly. The building stopped vibrating a second later, leaving only the fading ring of the impact.

Tigris-Fury did not move.

Soren stared at the downed machine. “Tigris!” He ran forward, but the referee stepped between him and the boundary until the safety systems confirmed the field was clear.

Kura looked for Zerdata. The machine had dropped to one knee. Its arms hung at its sides, and the glow was gone. Its optics remained lit, but dimly.

“Zerdata, are you okay?”

“Energy reserves nearly depleted,” it replied. “I remain conscious.”

The referee checked the field and Tigris-Fury’s status. The AniBot was unresponsive and unable to continue. After confirming the safety systems had isolated its power, the referee raised a hand.

“Match to Kura Ato! The AniBots World Cup champion!”

The crowd erupted. The sound crashed across the Dome, but this time the floor did not move beneath them.

Kura barely heard it. He crossed the field as soon as the officials allowed him through and knelt beside Zerdata. “You did it. We won.”

“We won,” Zerdata answered, voice faint but certain.

Luk reached the edge of the field first, followed by Anda and Isabell. Luk had tears in his eyes and tried to hide them by laughing.

“You actually did it,” he said. “You made it to the top.”

Isabell stayed beside Kura, one hand resting lightly on his shoulder. “I knew you could.”

Kura turned toward her. “You were there the whole time.”

“I’ll always be there.”

Luk opened his mouth, but Anda nudged him in the ribs before he could tease her again.

Across the field, Soren had reached Tigris-Fury. He crouched beside the machine while the recovery crew inspected its damaged frame. The anger had left his face. In its place was disbelief.

“This...” he said, looking back at Zerdata. “This is the power of Gen 0?”

Kura had no answer. Dr. Thorne, seated near the Handler section, watched the readouts with the same uncertainty. His family’s records had explained the Ancient architecture, but not the ability Zerdata had just unleashed—or the berserk state that had preceded it.

The trophy ceremony began after the medical and engineering teams cleared the arena. Kura stood on the winner’s platform with Zerdata at his side, its depleted frame supported by a temporary brace. The World Cup trophy gleamed under the Dome’s restored lights.

When the announcer raised Kura’s arm, Sammy, Spy, and Slow led District 4’s cheers. Spy’s speaker squealed and cut out. He kept shouting anyway. Sammy waved their crooked banner over her head. Slow clapped with both hands, careful not to crush the sign.

Luk stood below the platform beside Isabell and Anda. He looked disappointed about his own loss, but proud of Kura. When Kura met his eyes, Luk gave him a firm nod.

Kura had become World Champion. He had also seen Zerdata enter a state that no one—not Kura, Zerdata, or Dr. Thorne—could explain. The Limit Break had ended it, but the memory sectors had opened, and the Ancient core had acted on a power no modern system could measure.

The trophy was warm in Kura’s hands. The glow had faded, but the memory protocol remained active on the diagnostic screen. Somewhere inside Zerdata, fragments of an older life had opened, and an ability with no place in the modern records had answered them.

Kura looked at Zerdata. “We’ll understand it together.”

“I will remember,” Zerdata said. “And I will tell you what I can.”

The cheers rolled through the Grand Dome. Beyond them, Kura could still feel the last tremor in the floor.

The World Cup was over. The mystery had only begun.
