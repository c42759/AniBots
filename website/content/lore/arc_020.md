### Chapter One: A Week of Repairs

One week after Zerdata first woke, the Ato Family Workshop sounded different.

The old frame still creaked when it moved. Its exposed joints still carried the marks of years in Ty’s closed office. But Kura had spent the week replacing the cracked shoulder bearing, balancing the spring-loaded legs, reinforcing two loose torso mounts, and fitting a temporary heat sink beneath the back plates. Most of all, he had rebuilt the connection between his ear link and Zerdata’s core. Their voices no longer broke into static or arrived a second too late.

“Can you hear me clearly?” Kura asked.

“Every word.” Zerdata’s voice came through his earbud, with the same warmth and edge as the speaker in its chest. “You are standing too close to the left side of the bench.”

Kura glanced down. “How can you tell?”

“You keep tapping your wrench against the edge.”

“I wasn’t.”

The wrench tapped once against the metal bench.

Zerdata’s ears twitched. “You were.”

Kura grinned. It was the first time the link had worked through an entire calibration without a dropped syllable. He checked the connection meter on his slate: steady, encrypted, and clear. No direct neural interface—those were forbidden—but a reliable voice link at last.

“Then let’s see if it holds up in a fight.”

Luk had already claimed the District 4 practice ring for the morning. He waited inside the safety barrier with Kabuto-Horn, whose heavy treads left faint marks on the padded floor. Isabell sat in the front row, her rabbit charm hanging from her bag.

“You sure you’re ready?” Luk called. “We can give Zerdata another week of gentle strolls.”

Zerdata lowered its head. “I would prefer the heavy one stop talking and start moving.”

“Finally,” Luk said. “It’s learning from me.”

The safety field hummed around the ring. Kura adjusted his earbud and gave Zerdata a quick nod.

At Luk’s signal, Kabuto-Horn charged.

Its treads roared across the floor, twin-pronged horn lowered. The pneumatic arm pulled back for a crushing swing. Zerdata sprang to one side, and this time its legs landed together. The new bearing held. It slipped past the horn, turned on its heel, and struck Kabuto-Horn’s shoulder plate with the resonant claw.

The blow rang through the ring.

Kabuto-Horn rocked but stayed upright. Luk drove it around in a tight arc and brought the chitin guard up between them. Zerdata’s sonic pulse burst against the shield in a blue-white ripple. Kabuto-Horn pushed through the fading wave and jabbed with its piston arm.

“Kura, it’s closing!” Luk shouted.

“I hear it,” Kura replied.

He didn’t need to raise his voice. Through the ear link, Zerdata heard him clearly. It also heard the small change in Kabuto-Horn’s tread rhythm as Luk shifted weight to the left.

“Let it pass!”

Zerdata waited until the piston was almost on it. Then it ducked beneath the punch and sprang behind Kabuto-Horn. The new leg balance carried it cleanly through the turn. Its claw rapped against the exposed joint below the armor, and its sonic emitter sent a short pulse into the tread assembly.

Kabuto-Horn lurched. One tread spun in place.

Luk pulled it out of the turn, but Zerdata was already moving. It darted in, tapped the side of the shell, and sprang back before the piston could swing around.

“Now!” Kura called.

Zerdata released a second pulse. The blast struck the forearm guard and shoved Kabuto-Horn sideways. Its treads squealed. Luk fought for balance, but the heavy frame rolled onto one knee and stopped against the padded boundary.

The practice field went quiet.

Luk looked at the position, then laughed. “All right. You got us.”

Kura held up a hand. “Everyone good?”

“Fine,” Luk said, patting Kabuto-Horn’s shell. “A little embarrassed, but fine.”

Zerdata stood beside him, ears upright. “Your machine is very durable.”

“It has to be. Someone keeps trying to knock it over.” Luk climbed out of the ring and bumped Kura’s shoulder. “That was a real win. The new link worked?”

“Every word.”

“And the repairs?”

“Held.” Kura let himself smile. A week ago, Zerdata had struggled to cross a room without a servo hitch. Today, it had dodged, turned, and struck without fighting its own frame. “I think we’re getting there.”

Isa came over, bright-eyed. “You two moved like you already knew what the other was going to do.”

“We’re still learning,” Kura said.

“Then I hope I get to see the next one.” She glanced at him, then quickly at Luk. “I mean, if you’re going to practice again.”

Luk grinned. “We’ll make sure you get front-row seats.”

Outside the ring, a small boy hurried past them with a tool case clutched to his chest. He kept looking over his shoulder toward the salvage lanes.

“Slow’s down there,” he said to another kid. “He’s making people fight Honey-Beam again.”

Luk’s grin vanished. Kura looked toward the lanes.

“Who’s Slow?” Isa asked.

“Someone who thinks being big means everyone has to do what he says,” Kura replied. “Let’s go see what’s happening.”

### Chapter Two: The Muscle

Sammy, a sixteen-year-old girl Kura had known around District 4 for years, led the Trouble Crew. They were not friends. She was not there that afternoon, but Slow was acting on her orders.

They found Slow beside a salvage platform where younger handlers brought machines for repairs. He stood broad-shouldered in his bulky jacket and knit cap, hands in his pockets. His sleepy expression made him look almost harmless.

A boy no older than twelve stood in front of him, pale and gripping a small blue AniBot by its shoulder. Slow’s Honey-Beam occupied the lane between them. The heavy frame’s three pulse barrels pointed down, but its armored bulk filled the only easy way out.

“Sammy said you have to battle,” Slow told the boy. His voice was calm. “If you win, you can keep your parts. If you don’t, you give them to us.”

“I don’t want to fight,” the boy said.

“Then you’ll lose the parts anyway.”

Kura stepped between them. “He said no.”

Slow looked at him. “Sammy told me to get the parts.”

“Then tell Sammy he can come ask for them herself.”

Honey-Beam’s barrels lifted. Luk moved to Kura’s side, but Kura kept his eyes on Slow.

“Slow, you don’t have to do this.”

“I said I got him.” Slow looked down at the frightened boy. “Same pace. Same people. Always here.”

It sounded like a promise to his friend, not a threat. Kura understood that. It didn’t make the boy any less afraid.

“Then be here for him,” Kura said. “Let him go.”

Slow hesitated. Honey-Beam’s barrels tracked the movement. The boy slipped behind Kura and ran toward the salvage platform.

Slow’s face tightened. “I can’t just let you tell me what to do.”

“You don’t have to.” Kura stepped into the open space. “If you want to prove something, battle me instead.”

Luk leaned toward him. “You just won a fight. You don’t need to take every challenge.”

“I know.” Kura kept his voice low. “But that kid does need an out.”

Slow looked toward the boy, then back at Kura. “One battle. Just us.”

The nearby handlers drew back to clear a patch of packed earth. There was no official ring, only an old safety projector built into the salvage platform. Kura checked that it was active and waited for Slow to confirm.

Honey-Beam fired first. Three pulse shots thudded across the dirt. Zerdata leapt sideways as the first burst cracked the ground. The second shot clipped its thigh plate. The repaired leg buckled, caught, and carried it behind a low stack of scrap.

“Zerdata?” Kura asked through the link.

“Still moving.”

Honey-Beam’s third shot struck the scrap stack, showering the ground with sparks. Then a stream of sticky resin sprayed across the escape route, spreading into a glossy web that hardened as it cooled.

“Don’t cross that,” Kura warned. “It’ll bind your legs.”

Zerdata tilted its ears. It listened past the barrels’ metallic recoil to the pressure building in Honey-Beam’s resin tank.

“Left barrel is loading,” it said.

“Can you get close?”

“I can try.”

Kura gave one short command. “Go.”

Zerdata sprang over a narrow strip of resin as Honey-Beam fired again. The shot missed by a hand’s width. Zerdata landed near the machine’s flank, but Honey-Beam turned with surprising speed and swept its armored shoulder into the fox’s side.

Zerdata skidded. The heat sink kept its torso temperature steady; the new bearing absorbed the shock. It rolled to its feet as Honey-Beam’s barrels swung back.

“Watch the recoil,” Kura called. “It leans right after the burst.”

Zerdata heard the barrels fire. It darted left, then cut sharply right as Honey-Beam’s weight shifted. The triple burst passed behind it. Zerdata slid beneath the nearest barrel and drove a focused sonic pulse into the cluster of exposed feed lines.

The pulse vibrated through the metal. Honey-Beam’s next shot sputtered and died. The heavy AniBot tried to back away, but one tread caught in the resin it had sprayed. Its balance tipped toward the safety field.

“Stop!” Slow called.

Zerdata sprang clear. Honey-Beam slumped onto its side, its barrels dark. The safety field caught the impact and kept the machine from taking serious damage.

Kura lowered his hand. “You okay?”

Slow walked over to check his bot. “Yeah. Honey-Beam’s all right.” He looked toward the platform, where the younger boy had returned with his blue AniBot. “I was just doing what Sammy said.”

“Maybe next time ask what you want to do,” Kura said.

Slow rubbed the back of his neck. “I’ll think about it.”

The boy held out a small wrench to Kura. “Thanks.”

“You should keep that,” Kura said. “You’ll need it for your bot.”

The boy nodded and hurried off, this time without looking over his shoulder.

Slow helped Kura scrape the resin from Honey-Beam’s tread. He didn’t apologize, exactly, but he stayed to clear the lane and returned the parts the boy had been threatened into surrendering.

By sundown, the story had already reached Sammy.

### Chapter Three: The Scout

The next day, Kura heard Spy before he saw him.

“—and I’m telling you, it’s a known fact that Kura’s fox can only dodge left. I have records. I have diagrams. I have a scanner, which is almost the same as having records—”

Spy stood at the mouth of a narrow service alley with his yellow goggles pushed up on his forehead. A group of younger children stood boxed in between him and the wall. His AniBot, Cyan, hovered a few paces away, its compact frame swiveling as its firing ports tracked the group.

“I’m not asking you to hand over your parts,” Spy said quickly. “I’m asking you to battle for them. It’s a very different thing.”

“They don’t want to,” Kura said.

Spy jumped and nearly dropped his scanner. “Kura! I was just explaining the rules.”

“There aren’t any rules that make them fight you.”

Spy’s mouth opened, then shut. He looked at the kids and then at Cyan. “Sammy says if we let people ignore us, nobody will take the crew seriously.”

“You could try being someone they want to listen to.”

Spy flushed. “I’m not scared of you.”

“I didn’t say you were.”

“I know your weak points. I know your ear link drops out when Zerdata turns too fast. I know you favor your right side. I know the bot—” He swallowed. “Anyway, I have a lot of information.”

Kura looked at the scanner dangling from Spy’s hand. “Then you know the ring over there has a safety field.”

Spy straightened. “Of course.”

“Fight me. Leave them out of it.”

Spy glanced at the children, who hurried away as soon as Kura stepped aside. He squared his shoulders. “Fine. Cyan can handle one AniBot.”

They moved to an empty patch of the salvage court. Kura checked the safety field and the ear link. Zerdata’s voice came through cleanly.

“Can you hear me?”

“Yes.”

“Then keep listening.”

Cyan opened fire as soon as the field activated. Short bursts snapped across the court, driving Zerdata behind a stack of cargo drums. Spy darted along the opposite edge, calling out every move he thought he saw.

“He’ll come out on the left! No, right! I mean—Cyan, watch both sides!”

The shots swept over the drums. Zerdata waited, ears angling toward Cyan’s cooling vents and the slight pitch change before each burst. The rounds came in a rhythm, but the pauses were not where Spy expected them to be. Cyan’s weapons overheated between bursts; its right port took longer to reset.

“Now,” Kura said.

Zerdata sprang from cover just as Cyan’s left port fired. It dodged the first burst, twisted under the second, and kept moving while Spy shouted three conflicting directions. Cyan rotated to track it. Zerdata released a sonic pulse toward the ground. The shockwave skipped across the hard surface and struck Cyan’s hovering stabilizers from below.

Cyan wobbled. Its next burst went wide, punching harmlessly into the safety barrier.

Zerdata crossed the gap in a quick dash and tapped the emitter housing with its resonant claw. The firing ports folded shut.

Spy stared at the silent bot. “I thought you’d go left.”

“I heard you say that,” Zerdata replied. “So I did not.”

Kura raised his hand. “That’s enough.”

Spy lowered his scanner. His confidence had gone, leaving embarrassment behind. “You didn’t have to make it look easy.”

“It wasn’t easy,” Kura said. “You nearly hit the barrier with those shots.”

Spy glanced at the children down the lane. “They’re already gone.”

“They didn’t want to be here.”

Spy shoved the scanner into his sling bag. “Sammy’s going to say I lost because I didn’t have enough information.”

“Maybe tell her what actually happened.”

Spy didn’t answer. He walked away, muttering to himself about blind spots and misleading rumors. Kura watched until he turned the corner.

“Do you think he understood?” Luk asked from behind them. He had arrived in time to see the end.

“I think he understood that he lost,” Kura said. “The rest might take longer.”

### Chapter Four: The Ambush

On the third day, Kura and Luk found two young handlers trapped in the old loading yard.

The kids had set up a small repair table beneath the remains of a freight canopy. One of their AniBots was a wheeled courier frame; the other had a patchwork shield made from salvaged plastic. Spy stood near the yard entrance, talking fast and pointing at them. Slow waited beside Honey-Beam, blocking the nearest path out.

“We’re not taking their machines,” Spy was saying. “We’re just testing them. That’s how you know if they’re any good.”

“They said they don’t want to battle,” Luk called.

Spy turned. “That’s what they said yesterday, too. Then Kura challenged me, so I’m making the same offer.”

“You’re making them fight,” Kura said. “That isn’t an offer.”

Slow looked uncomfortable. “Sammy said don’t let them leave.”

“Then Sammy can come say it to us.” Luk stepped beside Kura. Kabuto-Horn rolled up behind him, heavy treads grinding over the yard’s cracked concrete.

Spy pointed at Kura. “You and Luk think you can fix everything by showing up together?”

“No,” Kura said. “We can just make sure nobody gets cornered alone.”

Spy’s hand snapped toward Cyan. Slow gave a tired sigh and raised Honey-Beam’s barrels. The two young handlers backed away from the repair table.

Kura raised his voice. “Get behind the cargo barrier!”

They ran.

Cyan’s first burst struck the ground in front of them. Kabuto-Horn charged across the yard, its guard plate raised. The second burst hammered against the shield and scattered sparks. Luk kept the heavy bot between Cyan and the children while Kura led Zerdata around the side.

Honey-Beam sprayed resin across the concrete, cutting off the direct route to Cyan. Zerdata leapt over the sticky band, but Spy had predicted the move from the previous day and swept Cyan’s fire toward the landing point.

“Kura, down!” Luk shouted.

Zerdata folded low. The burst passed overhead. Kabuto-Horn rammed Honey-Beam from the side, forcing the heavier machine to turn away from the children. Honey-Beam’s armored shoulder hit the ground with a bang.

Luk and Kabuto-Horn held its attention. Kura heard Cyan’s firing ports cycle and Zerdata’s feet strike the concrete. Through the clean ear link, every sound arrived where it belonged.

“Cyan’s right emitter is lagging,” Zerdata said.

“Can you reach it?”

“With cover.”

“Luk!” Kura called. “Keep Honey-Beam facing you!”

“Gladly!” Luk shouted.

Kabuto-Horn planted its treads and shoved its horn under Honey-Beam’s arm, levering the heavy bot sideways. Honey-Beam fired, but the pulse blasts struck the safety barrier. Its resin launcher swung around to trap Kabuto-Horn’s treads.

Zerdata darted past the spray and crossed behind Cyan. It struck the ground with a short sonic pulse. The vibration traveled beneath Cyan’s hover assembly, making the frame dip. The lagging emitter swung too far, and Zerdata tapped its casing with the resonant claw.

Cyan’s shots stopped.

Spy scrambled to grab its controls, but Luk’s voice cut across the yard. “Slow, look around. The kids are already safe.”

Slow turned. The two younger handlers were crouched behind the cargo barrier, their machines still beside them. Slow lowered Honey-Beam’s barrels.

“Sammy will be mad,” Spy said.

“Probably,” Kura replied. “But the kids are safe.”

The safety field dimmed. Luk eased Kabuto-Horn back, and Kura helped Slow free Honey-Beam’s tread from the resin. Spy retrieved Cyan’s scanner. His shoulders slumped, but he didn’t try to restart the fight.

“You two could’ve gone home,” Kura said to the younger handlers. “You don’t owe anyone a battle.”

One of them nodded. “We just wanted to fix our bots.”

“Then do that,” Luk said. “We’ll stay until you’re ready to leave.”

The four friends spent the rest of the afternoon helping the kids replace a cracked wheel and straighten the scrap shield. Spy lingered at the edge of the yard, watching. Slow stayed to scrape resin from the concrete. Neither said much.

By evening, the Trouble Crew had lost two fights and a little of its hold on the neighborhood.

### Chapter Five: The Leader

Sammy came looking for Kura on the fourth afternoon.

She found him at the canal-side plaza with Luk and Isa. Her teal bomber jacket hung open, and she chewed gum hard enough to make the pink bubble pop against her cheek. The smiley-face badge on her sleeve was scratched but bright.

“Two of my friends,” she said, pointing at Kura, “and you think you can walk around District 4 like you own it?”

“I’m not asking anyone to follow me.”

“You made Slow look stupid. You embarrassed Spy.”

“They were forcing younger kids into battles.”

“They’re my crew.” Sammy snapped her fingers. Volt-Pouncer leapt down from a low wall beside her. The cheetah-like frame landed on all fours, its Plugtail twitching with bright electric arcs. “If you want to interfere, fight me.”

Kura looked at Luk and Isa. “Stay back. This one’s mine.”

“Don’t do anything reckless,” Luk said.

Sammy laughed. “You always listen when your friends tell you what to do?”

The plaza’s practice barrier was built into the paving stones. Kura switched it on and confirmed the boundary. Volt-Pouncer crouched, muscles of metal tightening beneath its frame. Zerdata lowered its ears and listened.

Sammy struck first. Volt-Pouncer shot forward in a burst of speed, feinted left with its Plugtail, then sprang right. Blue-white electricity snapped across the space where Zerdata had been. Zerdata ducked beneath the discharge, but Volt-Pouncer was already behind it, claws scraping across the back armor.

“Don’t let it settle!” Sammy shouted.

Volt-Pouncer pounced again. Zerdata dodged, but the electric wake clipped one leg and made the springer stutter. Kura’s new interface carried every sound, yet Sammy’s bot was fast enough to make the directions arrive at the edge of the moment.

Zerdata’s ears tracked the quick changes in Volt-Pouncer’s motor pitch. Its next leap carried more weight; the landing foot struck harder. Kura saw the opening at the same time.

“Let it come straight at you!”

Sammy’s grin sharpened. “You’re asking it to get hit?”

Volt-Pouncer lunged. Zerdata waited until the last instant, then pivoted around the charge. The cheetah frame struck the safety barrier shoulder-first. Zerdata pressed its sonic emitter close to the rear leg and released a narrow pulse. The shock disrupted the leg’s next push.

Volt-Pouncer stumbled. Sammy corrected fast, calling for a turn, but Zerdata was already moving. It slipped behind the bot, hooked the resonant claw beneath its shoulder plate, and pulled just enough to break its balance. Volt-Pouncer rolled onto its side and skidded to a stop.

Sammy’s gum bubble burst. She stared at the field, then at Kura.

“Call it,” Kura said.

Sammy lifted her wrist. The safety field faded. Volt-Pouncer’s eyes dimmed to a steady glow as it powered down its combat systems.

Kura won. He didn’t celebrate. He checked that the frame was safe, then backed away to give Sammy room.

“Not bad,” she said, forcing a grin. “But you still only beat me one-on-one.”

Spy arrived first, Cyan at his side. Slow followed with Honey-Beam. Sammy’s grin turned real again.

“Three against one,” she said. “Unless you’re afraid to keep going.”

“That isn’t a fair fight,” Isa said.

“District 4 isn’t fair,” Sammy replied.

Kura glanced at the younger handlers gathering at the plaza edge. He could refuse the challenge, but Sammy would keep using the crowd as an audience and the crew as a threat.

Before he could answer, a familiar clatter rolled over the paving stones.

“Three against one is boring,” Luk called. Kabuto-Horn came to a stop at his side. “Make it three against three.”

Isa stepped forward. For a moment, she looked as if she might retreat behind her brother. Then she reached for the small communicator at her belt.

“Swan-Grace,” she said. “I’m ready.”

Her AniBot swept into the plaza on quiet hover drives, feather-light armor catching the afternoon sun. A slender rapier arm glimmered as it came to rest beside her.

Sammy studied the three of them. “Fine. Three each.”

The safety barrier expanded around the plaza’s practice court. The crowd moved back behind the marked line. Kura, Luk, and Isa stood side by side. Zerdata’s ears rotated, taking in the three opposing machines: Volt-Pouncer’s sharp electric crackle, Cyan’s chattering emitters, Honey-Beam’s heavier pulse and resin systems.

“Keep the kids clear,” Kura said.

“On it,” Luk replied.

Isa nodded. “Swan-Grace and I can cover the side.”

The battle began with Cyan’s rapid fire. Kabuto-Horn met the first burst behind its chitin guard. Luk drove forward, drawing Honey-Beam’s attention. Swan-Grace rose over the firing line, gliding between the shots with a light pirouette. Its rapier flicked out and clipped Cyan’s right emitter, turning the next burst into a wild scatter against the barrier.

Volt-Pouncer darted under the distraction. It sprang toward Zerdata, electricity snapping around its tail. Zerdata twisted clear, but the charge scorched the edge of its shoulder plate.

“Fast one is coming back!” Zerdata called through Kura’s ear link.

“I hear it.”

Kura and Isa moved at once. Swan-Grace descended across Volt-Pouncer’s path, forcing Sammy’s machine to veer away from the exposed side. Zerdata sent a sharp sonic pulse into the paving stones. The shockwave rose beneath Volt-Pouncer’s landing and threw off its footing. The cheetah bot skidded, claws sparking.

Sammy shouted for it to recover. Slow answered by swinging Honey-Beam’s triple barrels toward Zerdata. Luk planted Kabuto-Horn between them. The heavy frame absorbed the pulse blasts, then drove its horn beneath Honey-Beam’s front armor and shoved the larger machine away from the group.

“Isa, left side!” Kura called.

“Swan-Grace, with me!” Isa said.

The rapier flashed. Swan-Grace cut through the resin tube Honey-Beam had aimed across the ground. The sticky stream splattered harmlessly against the barrier instead of trapping Kabuto-Horn’s treads.

Cyan recovered and fired again. Zerdata heard the lag in its damaged right emitter and darted into the gap between bursts. It struck the casing with its resonant claw. The weapon arm folded, disabled but intact.

Spy stared at Cyan. “I thought you said the right side was the weak side!”

“It was!” he blurted. “I mean, it is. I didn’t think he’d—”

“Spy!” Sammy snapped.

Volt-Pouncer bounded back into the fight. Sammy made it feint left, then whip around with a low electric sweep. Zerdata jumped over the first strike, but its landing was tight. Kabuto-Horn moved in front of it and took the next hit on its guard plate. The armor flashed with blue light.

Luk kept the heavy machine facing him. “Kura, now!”

Zerdata darted past Kabuto-Horn. Swan-Grace glided alongside it, drawing Volt-Pouncer’s attention with a precise rapier thrust. The cheetah bot twisted to avoid the blade. Zerdata used the opening to plant both feet and release a focused pulse at the ground beneath it.

Volt-Pouncer’s legs buckled. It slid into the soft edge of the safety field and stopped.

Sammy started forward, but Luk and Kabuto-Horn blocked the path. Honey-Beam tried to rise. Kabuto-Horn pressed its guard plate against the heavy bot’s shoulder, holding it in place without striking. Swan-Grace’s rapier rested inches from Cyan’s chest. Zerdata stood between Sammy and Volt-Pouncer, ready but still.

Kura lowered his hand. “That’s enough. Nobody needs to get hurt.”

For a second, Sammy looked ready to argue. Then she saw the kids watching from beyond the barrier—the same kids she had tried to frighten into cheering for her. Her shoulders sank.

“Fine,” she said. “We’re done.”

The field dimmed. Luk let Kabuto-Horn back away. Isa lowered Swan-Grace’s rapier, and Zerdata stepped aside to give Volt-Pouncer space to stand.

Sammy helped her bot up. Spy retrieved Cyan’s scanner. Slow checked Honey-Beam’s resin line. Their machines were scuffed, overheated, and still functional.

Kura looked at Sammy. “You don’t have to keep making people afraid of you.”

“I’m not afraid of anyone.”

“Then you don’t need to prove it by cornering kids.”

Sammy looked away. “We’re leaving.”

Slow paused. “Want help with the resin later?”

“Maybe,” Kura said. “If you’re helping because you want to.”

Slow gave a slow nod and followed Sammy.

Isa stayed by Swan-Grace, her cheeks pink from the battle and the attention. Luk nudged Kura with his elbow.

“Not bad, first team battle.”

Isa smiled. “I think we worked well together.”

“We did,” Kura said. “You were amazing.”

She looked down, smiling wider.

By evening, the younger handlers of District 4 were talking about the fight. Some came by the workshop to thank Kura. Others asked Luk how to find the public practice ring. Kura didn’t call himself their protector. He only made sure they knew where to find help, and that no one had the right to force them into a battle.

The Trouble Crew had been beaten, but the district’s problems were bigger than three people. As rumors of Kura’s wins spread through the salvage lanes, new faces began watching him from the edge of the crowd.
