## Visual & Mechanical Design Guidelines

Welcome to the canonical **AniBots Character Design Guidelines**. This document defines the visual, anatomical, and mechanical rules for designing AniBots across 2D concept art, 3D models, and animation rigs.

Inspired by modular chibi-mecha design principles, AniBots establishes its own distinct engineering philosophy: **autonomous 5-part modularity**, **gender-neutral under-skeleton anchoring**, and **integrated joint-coupler networking** without external wireframe skeletons.

## 1. Proportional Rule: The 100–120 cm Silhouette

AniBots stand between **100 cm and 120 cm tall** (roughly 1.0 m to 1.2 m). This scale places them at chest height to human teenagers (such as 16-year-old Kura Ato), reinforcing their identity as dynamic, physical partners rather than towering military mechs or tiny tabletop toys.

```text
AniBot Vertical Mass Distribution (100–120 cm):
┌─────────────────────────┐  ◄── 100–120 cm Total Height
│      HEAD & VISOR       │      ~28–32% Height (Enlarged expressive head, sensor crests)
├─────────────────────────┤
│      TORSO CHASSIS      │      ~22–25% Height (Dense core, rear chip port, motherboard)
├─────────────────────────┤
│   UPPER LEGS (STUBBY)   │      ~12–15% Height (Shortened femur linkages, high torque)
├─────────────────────────┤
│ LOWER LEGS & PROPULSION │      ~30–35% Height (Chunky boots, treads, wheels, 
└─────────────────────────┘                      or hover thrusters)
```

### Proportional Constraints

1. **The Chibi-Mecha Ratio**:
   - Head-to-body ratio is strictly **1:3.5 to 1:4**.
   - Heads are intentionally enlarged to house the sensory suite, tactical weapon caches, and expressive glowing visors.
2. **The "Trimming" Principle**:
   - Do NOT draw lanky human silhouettes dressed in armor.
   - **Upper Extremities (Biceps & Thighs)**: Kept short, stubby, and dense. Minimal exposed midriff or waistline.
   - **Lower Extremities (Forearms & Lower Legs)**: Carry physical visual weight. Heavy gauntlets, massive weapon barrels, articulated claws, and wide mobility platforms ground the silhouette.

## 2. Anatomical Modularity & Joint Couplers

Every AniBot frame is built upon a standardized mechanical interface that allows seamless swapping of any part in the garage.

### 1. Universal Magnetic Couplers
- Arm and leg joints do not use delicate pin hinges or organic ball sockets.
- Instead, joints use standardized magnetic ring couplers with visible copper coil rings and locking teeth.
- This mechanical rationale explains why any Left Arm can be instantly disengaged and swapped onto another chassis during garage maintenance.

### 2. Rear Anima Chip Port
- The Torso chassis features an armored rear port positioned between the shoulder blades.
- When an Anima Chip is slotted, an motorized dust-shutter seals the connector pins.
- A circular transparent viewport exposes the chip's central glowing diode, allowing Handlers to inspect the core's status and series color in real time.

## 3. Visual Materiality & Finish Guidelines

To ground AniBots in their 3047 AD setting, visual designs balance sleek futuristic eco-materials with rugged, industrial functionalism:

- **Primary Armor Plates**: Matte powder-coated composites, anodized alloy plating, and lightweight ceramic paneling.
- **Under-Skeleton Core**: Industrial gunmetal gray (`#2B2D30`) with visible wiring harnesses and hydraulic pistons.
- **Accents & Optical Elements**: High-contrast illuminated visors (cyan, amber, ruby, emerald) displaying digital emotion expressions and targeting vectors.
- **Wear & Degradation Visuals**: As parts sustain condition loss, 3D shaders reveal localized paint scuffs, exposed metal scratches, and subtle thermal scorching around exhaust ports.
