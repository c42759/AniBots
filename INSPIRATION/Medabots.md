# MEDABOTS / MEDAROTS

The design artwork of the Medabots (Medarot) franchise, originally created by manga artist Rin Horuma (Horumarin), features a distinct and highly influential approach to mechanical character design. Rather than relying on the heavy, intricate grit of traditional 1990s anime mecha, Medabots established a unique aesthetic footprint tailored for a boy-and-his-robot partner dynamic.

The primary artwork aspects that define the Medabots look consist of the following elements:

## 1. The Anatomy of Modular Chibi-Mecha

Instead of solid-steel giant robots, Medabots are roughly 1-meter-tall compact machines built on a distinct anatomical philosophy:

- The Tinpet Skeleton: The absolute base of any Medabot artwork is the Tinpet—a simplified, wireframe-like metallic skeleton with integrated muscle cables. This base structure is highly visible in concept art, establishing human-like athletic proportions but with simplified, jointed robotic limbs.

- Aggressive Chibi Proportions: The designs blend stylized, slightly "chibi" (super-deformed) aesthetics with functional mechanical design. Their heads are disproportionately large to accommodate expressive, glowing visor eyes, making them feel like genuine characters rather than mindless weapons.

- The "Four Medaparts" Principle: Every piece of official artwork is strictly divided into interchangeable components: Head, Right Arm, Left Arm, and Legs. This modular design dictates how artists draw them; joints are explicitly segmented so the armor looks like it can effortlessly click off and swap with other robots.

## 2. Biomorphic and Thematic Motifs

Medabots are famous for mapping literal, everyday real-world concepts directly onto robotic hardware.

- The Insectoid Pillars (Kabuto vs. Kuwagata): The entire franchise revolves around the visual duality of beetles. KBT-types (like Metabee) mimic the Japanese rhinoceros beetle with blocky, yellow/white armor and a central head horn that doubles as a missile launcher. KWG-types (like Rokusho) mimic stag beetles with sleek, white/blue armor and large head-mandibles acting as swords.

- Literal Anthropomorphism: Beyond insects, artwork features highly literal, charming visual puns. A nurse-type Medabot will feature a head shaped like a nursing cap, and a feline-type will incorporate cat ears that house radar equipment.

## 3. Clear Visual Lineage & Art Style

In the original concept art and the critically acclaimed anime iterations (directed by Tensai Okamura and animated by Bee Train/Production I.G), the rendering style is highly specific:

- Thin Line Art & Flat Colors: The aesthetic relies on remarkably thin, clean line art paired with relatively flat, slightly muted color palettes. It eschews heavy metallic gradients or complex shading, resulting in a clean, minimalist look reminiscent of Mamoru Hosoda’s early animation work.

- Geometric vs. Fluid Forms: While the mechanical parts are comprised of hard geometric shapes (hexagonal medals, cylinder gun barrels, and blocky shoulder pads), they are drawn with a fluid elasticity. This allows the robots to strike incredibly dynamic, organic, and human-like martial arts poses despite being made of "M Alloy" armor.

## 4. Evolution/Transition to 3D Games

An exploration of the core model design sheets for the mascot characters, Metabee and Rokusho, reveals how these frameworks seamlessly transitioned from flat 2D art into the dynamic 3D modeling language used in modern iterations like Medabots DS.

### 4.1. The Classic Design Sheets: Metabee & Rokusho

The original character model sheets for the classic mascots establish strict geometric benchmarks to maintain visual consistency across media.

- Metabee (KBT-00): Built entirely out of hard angles and heavy canisters. His model sheets emphasize a split-visor head, where his eyes sit deep inside a dark, hollow recess to maintain a fierce expression. His right-arm Revolver and left-arm Submachine Gun are drawn as heavy blocks offset by thin wrist joints. This contrast gives his small silhouette a silhouette that feels dense and top-heavy, perfectly matching his stubborn personality.

- Rokusho (KWG-00): Designed with an emphasis on fluid, triangular shapes. Rather than the squat, barrel-like chest of Metabee, Rokusho’s sheets highlight a tapered torso, long shoulder pauldrons, and elegant stag-beetle mandibles cresting his head. His arms feature asymmetrical weapon silhouettes—the Chanbara Sword on the right and the Picopeco Hammer on the left—which require artists to use distinct focal points depending on the angle he is facing.

### 4.2. The Leap to 3D Modeling (Medabots DS and Beyond)

Translating Rin Horuma’s ultra-thin line art into 3D polygon meshes required visual adjustments to prevent the robots from looking stiff or awkwardly proportioned.

- Exaggerated Volumetric Armor: In 2D illustrations, pieces like Metabee’s shoulder blocks were drawn flat. To make them look correct in 3D games like Medabots DS, modelers increased the roundness and thickness of the armor plates, giving the machines a tactile weight.

- Articulated Joints: Because the original design concept already separated the armor from the under-skeleton (Tinpet), rigging the 3D models was unusually straightforward. Modelers used the natural "ball-and-socket" gaps at the shoulders, elbows, and knees to hide digital joints, keeping the mechanical parts looking solid while allowing a full, organic range of movement.

- Cel-Shaded Visors & Textures: To honor the original clean aesthetic, modern games utilize a bright, cel-shaded art style. The digital models use flat color maps paired with high-contrast emissive textures on the eyes, making the visors pop against dark metallic faceplates just like the classic animation frames.

# CHARACTERS DESIGN GRUIDE LINES


## 1. The Proportional Rule (The "Trimming" Principle)

A common mistake when drawing a Medabot is making it look like a lanky human dressed in armor. The guidelines demand tightly packed, compact proportions:
-  The Chibi Anchor: Medabots stand between 75 to 100 cm tall. Their heads must be intentionally enlarged (often taking up 1/3 to 1/4 of their total height) to leave room for expressive visors.

- Minimalist Upper Extremities: The torso must be short and tight, leaving very little "midriff" or waist space exposed. Bicep and thigh segments must be significantly shortened and kept stubby, while the forearms, shoulders, and lower legs carry the physical visual weight.

## 2. Under-Skeleton Strictness (The Tinpet Constraint)

Every single Medabot armor design must be built strictly on top of an active Tinpet framework.

- Gender-Locked Framing: Designers must first choose a Male or Female Tinpet base. Male Tinpets use blockier, broader structures, while Female Tinpets utilize narrow shoulders, closely aligned hips, and a slightly sleeker core frame.

- Visible Joints & Exposed Cables: Armor plates should never realistically meld together. Designers must leave gaps at the shoulders, elbows, and knees to clearly expose the black, mechanical Muscle Cables underneath.

## 3. The Four Medaparts Modular Formula

A design cannot simply be an integrated robot outfit; it must be separated into four strictly distinct components that can be swapped out mid-battle:

- Head/Torso (1 Part): Houses the main sensory suite (horns, ears, hats) and must feature a distinct, protective slot in the upper back for the hexagonal Medal brain.

- Dual Arms (2 Parts): The left and right arm parts must terminate in explicit "weaponized hands" or action modules (e.g., guns, swords, shields, or claws). Even if they look symmetrical, they are drawn with subtle variances to denote independent functions.

- Legs/Base (1 Part): The lower body dictates the machine's movement type. It must explicitly follow a specific mobility class: Two-Legged (Bipedal), Multi-Legged (Insectoid), Wheeled, Tracked, Flying, or Subaquatic.

## 4. Direct Motif Transmutation

Medabots are conceptually defined by mapping a singular, recognizable motif (an insect, an animal, a profession, or a historical figure) into a completely robotic form.

- Literal Mechanical Translation: If the motif is an owl, the head piece must mimic the facial disk of an owl, and radar dishes might shape the wings. If it is a knight, the armor resembles stylized plate mail.
- Function Over Decoration: Decorative elements must serve an immediate weaponized or functional purpose. For example, a beetle's horn cannot just be ornament—it must visually function as a cannon barrel or a kinetic blade.

## 5. High-Contrast Visor Rule

Unlike traditional mecha that use tiny glowing slit-eyes, Medabots emphasize character personality through their visors.

- The Negative Space Receptacle: The faceplate must feature a dark recessed area. Within this hollow space, flat, glowing neon visors (usually green, red, or yellow) are placed. This design choice creates a high-contrast shadow line around the eyes, giving the robot a sharp, lively expression even when viewed from a distance or silhouette.
