Medabots: Metabee Version and Medabots: Rokusho Version are a pair of 2D top-down role-playing games released for the Game Boy Advance (GBA) by Natsume in 2002/2003. While they were the first Medabots RPGs to see an international release, they are actually enhanced remakes of Medarot 2, a Japan-exclusive title released for the Game Boy Color in 1999.

# The Story
The narrative closely parallels the popular Medabots anime series. You play as Ikki Tenryou, a young boy obsessed with "Robattling" who desperately wants a Medabot of his own. After a mix-up at a convenience store leaves him with an outdated set of Medaparts (either Metabee or Rokusho, depending on the version), Ikki's father gifts him a rare Medal he received from a mysterious stranger known as the Phantom Renegade.

With his Medabot finally assembled, Ikki sets out to climb the Robattle ranks, competing in tournaments, dealing with local bullies like the "Screws," and thwarting the world-domination plans of the nefarious Rubberobo Gang.

# Unique Gameplay and "Robattles"
The game is split into traditional RPG overworld exploration and a highly unique 3-on-3 turn-based combat system. Battles operate on a "relay run" mechanic similar to an Active Time Battle (ATB) system: Medabots start on their side of the screen and must physically sprint to a center line to execute their chosen command before running back to cool down.

Deep customization is the core of the game. A Medabot consists of a central Medal (which grants an ultimate attack called the "Medaforce") and four distinct Medaparts:

- Legs: Determine the Medabot's speed and mobility based on the battlefield's terrain.

- Left & Right Arms: Used for infinite standard attacks (shooting or melee).

- Head: Houses the most powerful attacks or utilities but has finite ammo.

The Win Condition: If a Medabot's Head part is destroyed, it ceases to function. You win a Robattle by destroying the Head of the opponent's designated "Leader" Medabot. As a reward, the loser must surrender one of their Medaparts to the victor.


# Metabee vs. Rokusho Differences

Like the Pokémon franchise, the two versions feature the same core storyline but contain version-exclusive Medals, collectable Medaparts, and minor boss variations. The biggest difference is your starter Medabot, which drastically changes the game's difficulty:

| Feature | Metabee Version | Rokusho Version |
|--------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Starter Model | KBT (Kabuto / Rhino Beetle) | KWG (Kuwagata / Stag Beetle) |
| Combat Style | Ranged firepower (rifles/missiles) | Close-quarters melee (swords/hammers) |
| Durability | High armor and defense | Fragile; relies heavily on evasion and traps |
| Difficulty | Normal: Heavy damage output makes early battles highly forgiving. | Hard: Rokusho's fragility makes the early game notoriously difficult until you can build up defensive parts. |

# Medaforce
The Medaforce is an ultimate attack and utility system that operates independently of the Medaparts your Medabot has equipped. Instead, these abilities are tied directly to your Medabot's central "brain"—its Medal.

## 1. Charging the Gauge
During a Robattle, you will notice a percentage gauge (the MF Gauge) for each of your Medabots. You cannot use a Medaforce until you have enough energy stored. The gauge fills in two primary ways:

- Taking Damage: Your Medabot automatically generates energy whenever an opponent hits it.

- Manual Charging: When it is your Medabot's turn, you can select the "Charge" command instead of a standard attack. The Medabot will sprint to the center line and spend its turn generating a large chunk of energy.

## 2. Unlocking Abilities

Every Medal in the game (Kabuto, Kuwagata, Tortoise, Bear, etc.) possesses exactly three unique Medaforce abilities.

- You don't start with them; they unlock progressively as your Medal gains experience and levels up from winning Robattles.

- The three abilities always unlock at Level 10, Level 30, and Level 60.

## 3. Executing a Medaforce

Once your gauge reaches the required threshold—which varies from 30% to 80% depending on the specific skill—you can unleash it.

- Bypasses Destroyed Parts: Because the power comes directly from the Medal, you can execute a Medaforce even if your Medabot's weapons (its arms) have been completely destroyed.

- Guaranteed Hits: Medaforce attacks bypass the opponent's evasion and standard Part Defense stats, dealing exact, unmitigated damage.

# Variety of Effects

While starter Medabots like Metabee and Rokusho have Medaforces focused on pure destruction, other Medals offer vital strategic utilities that can completely turn the tide of a Robattle:

- Offensive: The Kabuto Medal's Ultra Shot (Level 10) unleashes a massive blast by combining the power of all your currently equipped shooting parts.

- Defensive: The Knight Medal's Iron Wall (Level 10) drastically reduces incoming damage for a set time.

- Supportive: The Mermaid Medal's Total Recovery (Level 10) can actually repair and fully restore your damaged or destroyed Medaparts mid-battle.
